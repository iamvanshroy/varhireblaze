How to Tailor Your Resume to a Job in 15 Minutes

Tailoring your resume doesn't mean starting over for every job. With a solid base resume, you can adapt it in about fifteen minutes. Here's the routine.

Before you start: build a master resume

Keep one longer document with every project, internship, skill and achievement you have, each written as a strong bullet point. When you tailor, you choose from it rather than writing from scratch.

The 15-minute routine

Minutes 0–3: Read the job description twice

First for understanding, then with a highlighter. Mark the must-have skills, tools and the job title.

Minutes 3–6: Rewrite your summary

Use the job's title and two or three of its top skills that you genuinely have. For example: "Aspiring Data Analyst skilled in SQL, Excel and Power BI, with projects analysing sales and customer data."

Minutes 6–10: Reorder and swap bullets

Move the most relevant project or internship to the top. Swap in bullets from your master resume that use the same tools as the posting.

Minutes 10–12: Update the skills section

Put the posting's priority skills first. Remove unrelated ones that take up space.

Minutes 12–15: Check the match

Run your resume and the job description through a checker. Look at the missing-skills list, fix anything honest you overlooked, and export as PDF with a clear file name.

Track it: keep a simple sheet with the company, role, date applied, and which version you sent. When you get an interview, you'll know exactly what they read.

Is it worth it?

Yes. A targeted resume is more likely to match recruiter searches and reads as more relevant to the person reviewing it. Ten tailored applications usually beat fifty identical ones.
