Should Your Resume Be One Page or Two?

It's one of the most-asked resume questions, and the honest answer depends on how much relevant experience you have.

One page is right if…

  • You're a student, fresher or have under about five years of experience.
  • You're changing careers and most of your history isn't relevant.
  • You're applying for internships or entry-level roles.

Recruiters screening early-career candidates want your best evidence fast. A second page usually just contains weaker material.

Two pages can make sense if…

  • You have many years of experience directly related to the role.
  • You're in a field where publications, patents or certifications are expected.
  • Cutting would remove information the employer specifically asked for.

Even then, page one must stand on its own. Assume some readers never reach page two.

How to cut down to one page

  1. Remove school-level details once you're in college, except standout achievements.
  2. Drop the objective and replace it with a two-line summary.
  3. Cut bullets that don't have a result, or merge two weak ones into one strong one.
  4. Keep only relevant certificates. Three strong ones beat twelve short online courses.
  5. Remove "References available on request" and personal details like date of birth.
  6. Tighten wording. "Was responsible for managing" becomes "Managed".
Don't shrink the font to cheat. Below about 10pt, or with tiny margins, the resume becomes hard to read. Cut content instead.

Bottom line

Default to one page early in your career. Move to two only when every line on the second page would make a recruiter more interested, not less.
