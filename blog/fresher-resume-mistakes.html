10 Resume Mistakes Freshers Make (and How to Fix Them)

As a fresher you're competing with hundreds of people who have the same degree. Small mistakes are what separate "shortlisted" from "ignored". Here are the ones that show up most, and how to fix each one.

1. Writing an objective instead of a summary

"Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation…" tells the recruiter nothing. Replace it with two lines about what you can do: "Computer science student with hands-on projects in Python and machine learning; built a resume analysis tool used by 500+ students."

2. Listing duties, not results

"Worked on the college fest website" becomes "Built the fest website in React; handled 2,000 registrations without downtime."

3. Hiding your projects at the bottom

For freshers, projects are your experience. Put them right after your summary, with a link to GitHub or a live demo.

4. A skills list with no proof

Listing 25 technologies looks like padding. Keep the ones you can talk about in an interview, and make sure each important one appears in a project or internship bullet.

5. Going past one page

Unless you have substantial work experience, one page is the standard for freshers. Cut school-level achievements and unrelated certificates first.

6. Fancy templates that break ATS parsing

Two-column designs with skill bars and icons look good to you, but they often turn into scrambled text in an ATS. A clean single-column layout is safer.

7. An unprofessional email or file name

Use a simple email built from your name, and name the file Firstname_Lastname_Resume.pdf, not final_final_v3.pdf.

8. Including personal details nobody asked for

Father's name, date of birth, marital status, religion and a full home address aren't needed for most private-sector jobs today. City and state are enough.

9. Typos and inconsistent formatting

Mixed date formats, random bold text and spelling errors signal carelessness. Read it aloud once, and ask a friend to read it too.

10. Sending the same resume to every job

A five-minute tweak to the summary and skills for each role can noticeably improve how well you match. Use the job description as your guide.

One-evening plan: rewrite your summary, move projects up, add a number to every bullet you can, and run the result through a resume checker against a real job posting.
