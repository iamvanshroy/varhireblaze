Are Browser Games Safe? A Simple Checklist

Good browser games are one of the safest ways to play online: nothing is installed, and the browser keeps each website in its own sandbox. But not every game site plays fair. Use this checklist to spot the good ones.

Green flags ✅

  • It plays right in the page. No installers, no "download our launcher".
  • It uses HTTPS. Look for the padlock or "secure" icon in the address bar.
  • It explains what it stores. A clear privacy page that tells you about cookies, ads and saved scores.
  • No account needed to play. Optional nicknames for leaderboards are fine.
  • Ads are clearly ads. They're separate from the game and don't pretend to be game buttons.

Red flags 🚩

  • "Your device is infected!" pop-ups or fake virus warnings. Close the tab.
  • Requests to install an extension or app just to play a simple game.
  • Asking for your camera, microphone, contacts or location when the game has no reason to use them.
  • Fake download or "Play" buttons that are really ads.
  • Asking for passwords, OTPs or card details to "unlock" a free game.
  • Notification prompts the moment the page opens.

Privacy basics

Most web games save your progress in your browser's own storage (localStorage). That data stays on your device. If a game has online leaderboards, check what's sent: ideally just a nickname you choose and your score. Never use your full name, phone number or email as a public nickname.

For parents and teachers

Browser games are great for short breaks, but it's still worth checking a site's content and ads first, setting time limits, and preferring sites with no chat between strangers.
