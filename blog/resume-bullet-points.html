How to Write Resume Bullet Points That Get Interviews

Recruiters often skim a resume in under a minute. Your bullet points are what they actually read, so each one has to earn its line.

The formula: Action + What + Result

Start with a strong verb, say what you did and with what, then say what changed because of it.

Action verb + task, with tools + measurable result
"Automated weekly attendance reports using Python and Google Sheets API, saving the team about 3 hours a week."

Before and after

  • Before: Responsible for social media.
    After: Planned and posted 3 Instagram reels a week for the college club, growing followers from 800 to 2,400 in four months.
  • Before: Worked on a machine learning project.
    After: Trained a logistic regression model on 10k loan records to predict defaults, reaching 84% accuracy on held-out data.
  • Before: Helped customers at internship.
    After: Resolved 25–30 customer tickets a day over chat, with a 92% satisfaction rating.
  • Before: Made a website.
    After: Built and deployed a responsive event site in HTML, CSS and JavaScript on Vercel, handling 1,200 sign-ups.

Where to find numbers when you think you have none

  • Scale: how many users, rows of data, people in the team, events, pages.
  • Time: how long something took, or how much time you saved.
  • Money: budget handled, sponsorship raised, cost reduced.
  • Quality: accuracy, ratings, error rates, grades.
  • Frequency: per day, per week, per month.

Estimates are fine when they're honest and you can explain them, for example "about 200 users".

Strong verbs to start with

Built, Designed, Launched, Automated, Analysed, Reduced, Increased, Led, Organised, Negotiated, Wrote, Trained, Deployed, Improved, Resolved.

Avoid weak starts like "Responsible for", "Helped with" and "Worked on". They describe being present, not achieving something.

How many bullets?

Three to five for each role or project is plenty. Put the most impressive one first, because many readers stop after it.
