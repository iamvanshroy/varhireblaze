A Work-From-Home Routine That Actually Works

Working from home removes the commute, but it adds something else: everything in your house wants your attention. Without the structure of an office, days blur, breaks disappear and work leaks into the evening. Here is a simple routine that fixes most of that, whether you're a fresher on your first remote job or job hunting from your bedroom.

1. Start the day like you're leaving the house

Get up at roughly the same time, get dressed (yes, really), and do a five-minute "commute": a short walk, making tea, or stretching. This tells your brain that work is starting, which makes it easier to begin.

2. Give work one fixed place

A small desk in a corner beats working from bed. When work has one place, it's easier to focus while you're there and easier to stop when you leave it. Keep that spot simple: laptop, notebook, water, and nothing else you'll be tempted to fiddle with.

3. Plan three things, not thirty

Write down the three tasks that would make today a good day. Do the hardest one first, before you open chat apps. Everything else is a bonus.

4. Batch the small interruptions

  • Messages and email: check them at set times (say 11, 2 and 5) instead of all day.
  • Deliveries and chores: deal with them in your breaks, not in the middle of focused work.
  • Calls: keep your camera and mic ready before the meeting starts, not after you've joined.
Tip: Turn off notifications on your phone while you're doing deep work. Put the phone in another room if you have to. Most messages can wait 45 minutes.

5. Take real breaks

Every 45–60 minutes, stand up. Refill your water, look out of a window, or play one short game round. A break that ends is the key: pick something with a clear finish, not an endless scroll.

6. Have a hard stop

Decide when work ends and close the laptop at that time. Write tomorrow's three tasks first, so you don't carry them in your head all evening.

Remote work is a skill on your resume too

If you've worked or studied remotely, recruiters care about the same things this routine builds: managing your own time, communicating clearly in writing and getting things done without supervision. Say so on your resume with a specific example.

Take a 3-minute break

Keep a meeting, a coffee, a plant, a cat and a delivery alive at the same time. It's harder than it sounds.
