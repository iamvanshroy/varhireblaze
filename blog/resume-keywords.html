How to Find the Right Keywords for Your Resume

Keywords are the words a recruiter is likely to search for: skills, tools, job titles and qualifications. Getting them right is the single biggest lever on whether your resume shows up. Here's a repeatable method that takes about ten minutes per job.

Step 1: Collect two or three similar job postings

One posting can be quirky. Three postings for the same role show you what the market consistently asks for. Copy their text into one document.

Step 2: Highlight four kinds of words

  • Hard skills and tools: Excel, SQL, Figma, Python, Tally, Google Ads.
  • Job titles: "Business Analyst", "Frontend Developer", "Content Writer".
  • Domain terms: "B2B", "fintech", "supply chain", "REST APIs".
  • Qualifications: degrees, certifications, languages.

Soft skills like "communication" matter less as search terms. Show them through results instead of listing them.

Step 3: Rank by frequency

Anything that appears in all three postings is a must-have. Anything in one posting is a nice-to-have.

Step 4: Check honestly what you can claim

Only add keywords you could defend in an interview. If you've never used Tableau, don't list it. Learn the basics and build a small project first, then add it.

Step 5: Place them where they count

  • Summary: your target job title and two or three core skills.
  • Experience and projects: the tool inside a result. For example: "Cleaned 50k rows of sales data in Python (pandas) and built a Power BI dashboard used in weekly reviews."
  • Skills section: a clean list, grouped, such as Languages / Tools / Platforms.

Step 6: Match exact phrasing

Searches are literal. If the posting says "JavaScript", don't only write "JS". If it says "project management", include that phrase rather than only "managed projects". It's fine to include both forms once, like "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)".

What not to do

Don't paste a block of keywords at the bottom, and don't hide text in white font. Both are easy for recruiters to spot and make the resume read worse to the person who decides.
