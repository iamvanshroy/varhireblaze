Resume vs CV: What's the Difference?

"Send your CV" and "send your resume" are often used as if they mean the same thing, and depending on where you are, they sometimes do. Here's how to tell what the employer actually wants.

The traditional difference

  • Resume: a short (usually one to two pages), targeted summary of your skills, experience and achievements, tailored for a specific job.
  • CV (curriculum vitae): a longer, complete record of your academic and professional history, including publications, research, teaching, conferences and awards. It grows over your career.

How it varies by country

  • India: "CV" and "resume" are used interchangeably in most private-sector hiring. Both usually mean a one- or two-page document. A long academic CV is expected mainly for research, PhD and faculty roles.
  • USA and Canada: "resume" is the norm for jobs. "CV" means the long academic version.
  • UK, Europe, Australia: "CV" is the everyday word for what Americans call a resume, a short two-page document.

Which one should you send?

  • For corporate, startup and most government-company jobs, send a concise, targeted resume, whatever the posting calls it.
  • For research positions, university admissions, fellowships and teaching roles, send a full academic CV.
  • If unsure, look at the posting's requirements. If it asks for a publication list or research statement, it wants a CV.

What both have in common

Either way, the document should be easy to scan, use clear headings, and be truthful. If it goes through an online portal, it also needs to be readable by an ATS, so stick to a simple layout and text-based PDF.
