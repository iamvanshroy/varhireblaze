Stuck in Traffic Again? How to Survive the Daily Commute

Ninety minutes in stop-and-go traffic can leave you tired and irritated before you've done a minute of work. You can't control the jam, but you can control a lot about how the commute feels. Here's what helps.

Why traffic is so stressful

  • No control. You can't make the cars move, and feeling helpless is stressful in itself.
  • Uncertainty. Not knowing if you'll be late is often worse than actually being a bit late.
  • Constant small decisions. Brake, move, switch lanes or not. They add up.

Before you leave

  • Leave 10–15 minutes earlier than you think you need. The buffer removes most of the "am I late?" stress.
  • Check the route on a maps app before you set off, not while driving.
  • Tell people if you'll be late, early, so you're not stressing about it all the way.

Make the time yours

If you're driving, keep your eyes on the road, but your ears are free:

  • Podcasts or audiobooks about your field. Thirty minutes a day adds up to many books a year.
  • Practise interview answers out loud. Your car is the one place nobody can hear you.
  • Music you like, and nothing that makes you more wound up.

On public transport you can also read, plan your day or reply to messages, so the work day starts lighter.

Stay safe: Never type, scroll or play on your phone while driving, even in slow traffic. Set up your audio before you start the car.

Lane-hopping rarely wins

Switching lanes constantly usually saves very little time and adds a lot of stress and risk. Pick a sensible lane and stay calm. If you want to hop lanes, there's a game for that.

Can you change the commute itself?

If you're job hunting, commute time is worth asking about. Some companies offer hybrid days, flexible hours (so you can travel outside rush hour) or office locations near metro lines. Mention it politely once you have an offer, not in the first interview.

Take a 3-minute break

Hop into whichever lane is moving before Vansh's frustration meter fills up. Way more fun than the real thing.
